# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here.

## [1.0.0] - 2026-05-12

### Released
- Published **contract-obligation-command-center** as the legal-ops flagship in the portfolio.
- Packaged obligation queues, renewal pressure, owner lanes, and escalation logic into a practical review system.
- Clarified the repo around execution risk in contracts, not around generic document analysis.

### Why this mattered
- Contracts usually fail operationally because ownership and timing break down, not because the clause text is literally missing.
- Legal and procurement teams often inherit fragmented obligation tracking across inboxes, spreadsheets, and CLM exports.
- This release made the repo legible to legal ops, procurement, and business operations audiences.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-02-14

### Shipped
- Standardized the first internal model for obligations, milestones, approval blockers, and renewal pressure.
- Added queue views that emphasized what should move now and why.

## [Prototype] - 2025-05-10

### Built
- Built the first prototype around obligation visibility, ownership gaps, and milestone escalation.
- Tested whether the system could make contract execution risk easier to review than flat CLM lists.

## [Design Phase] - 2024-02-02

### Designed
- Chose an operations framing over a contract-analysis framing.
- Put ownership, deadlines, and next-best action at the center of the UI and model.
- Kept the repo oriented around legal execution, not legal theory.

## [Idea Origin] - 2023-03-30

### Observed
- The idea emerged from seeing how often obligations were technically known but operationally under-owned.
- The missing layer was a control surface for clause-driven work after signature.
